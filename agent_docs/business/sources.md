# Business-case source register

Accessed August 4, 2026 unless noted. Prefer the primary source linked here over secondary summaries. Verify all current product pricing, laws, and program participation immediately before publication.

## Market population and spending

- [NCES, Parent and Family Involvement in Education: 2023](https://nces.ed.gov/pubs2024/2024113.pdf): official 2022–23 homeschool estimate; survey definitions and sampling caveats apply.
- [NCES homeschool Fast Facts](https://nces.ed.gov/fastfacts/display.asp?id=91): federal trend summary.
- [U.S. Census Household Pulse Survey, Cycle 09](https://www.census.gov/data/tables/2024/demo/hhp/cycle09.html): August–September 2024 experimental household estimates.

- [Pew overview of U.S. homeschooling](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/02/20/a-look-at-homeschooling-in-the-us/): accessible summary of federal data and parent motivations.

- [NCSL ESA policy scan](https://www.ncsl.org/education/education-choice-state-policy-scan-education-savings-accounts): state ESA program inventory.
- [EdChoice 2026 spending-share rankings](https://www.edchoice.org/2026-edchoice-spending-share-rankings/): modeled state choice-program spending; advocacy source.

## Tutoring and mastery evidence

- [Nickow, Oreopoulos, and Quan, AERJ 2024](https://doi.org/10.3102/00028312231208687): peer-reviewed meta-analysis of 89 randomized tutoring trials; pooled effect 0.288 SD.
- [NSSA summary of Nickow et al.](https://nssa.stanford.edu/studies/promise-tutoring-prek-12-learning-systematic-review-and-metaanalysis-experimental-evidence): accessible study summary.
- [Kraft, Schueler, and Falken, tutoring at scale](https://doi.org/10.26300/zygj-m525): expanded meta-analysis emphasizing policy-relevant scale effects.
- [EdResearch for Action, high-impact tutoring design principles](https://edresearchforaction.org/research-briefs/design-principles-for-accelerating-student-learning-with-high-impact-tutoring/): synthesis of dosage and implementation features.
- [Guryan et al., AER 2023](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20210434): two Chicago randomized trials of intensive mathematics tutoring.
- [Bhatt et al., NBER 32510](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32510): randomized evaluation of a technology-supported tutoring model.
- [Carlana and La Ferrara online tutoring study](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Faer.20240401): randomized online tutoring evidence in Italy.
- [Bloom, 1984](https://doi.org/10.3102/0013189X013006004): original two-sigma paper; historical framing, not a modern product-effect estimate.
- [von Hippel, 2024](https://www.educationnext.org/two-sigma-tutoring-separating-science-fiction-from-science-fact): reconstruction and critique of Bloom's evidence.
- [Kulik, Kulik, and Bangert-Drowns, 1990](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543060002265): mastery-learning meta-analysis.
- [Slavin, 1987](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543057002175): stricter best-evidence review of mastery learning.
- [Muralidharan, Singh, and Ganimian, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20171112): randomized computer-adaptive instruction study.
- [Muralidharan and Singh, scale-up working paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34205): adaptive instruction at government-school scale.
- [Kraft, interpreting education effect sizes](https://doi.org/10.3102/0013189X20912798): effect-size interpretation framework.

## Source-quality rules for the deck

1. Use one source per claim and show the source year on the slide or in notes.
2. Prefer the peer-reviewed estimate when a working-paper estimate differs.
3. Describe NHERI and EdChoice figures as modeled/advocacy-source estimates where applicable.
4. Do not use vendor-authored efficacy as equivalent to independent research.
5. Treat unofficial competitor pricing as directional and omit it from the deck if it cannot be verified.
6. Refresh laws, product features, prices, and ESA program rules immediately before external use.
7. Preserve vintage caveats when combining public 2024 and private 2021–22 enrollment.
8. Record pilot nulls and adverse findings, not only positive outcomes.
