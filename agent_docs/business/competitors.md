# Competitive landscape

## Category definition

Primer competes with several product categories, not one direct equivalent. The correct question is not “Who else has an AI chat box?” It is “What stack does the adult assemble today to plan instruction, tutor the student, assess mastery, enforce habits, and document learning?”

## Competitive map

| Category | Examples | What customers buy | Primer response |
|---|---|---|---|
| General AI study tools | ChatGPT Study Mode, Gemini Guided Learning | Near-free explanations and study help | Do not compete on conversation alone; win on adult governance, curriculum, evidence, and continuity |
| AI tutors | Khanmigo, Synthesis Tutor, SchoolAI, Amira | Guided help or subject tutoring | Differentiate with grades 5–8 breadth, cross-subject habits, projects, and evidence per standard |
| Mastery systems | Math Academy, ALEKS, MATHia | Rigorous adaptive progression, usually one subject | Match rigor where offered; add cross-subject transfer and real-work evidence |
| Homeschool platforms | Time4Learning, Power Homeschool, Miacademy, Acellus | Broad, self-paced curriculum and automatic reports | Offer deeper diagnosis, correction, and adult control with less screen dependence |
| Off-screen curriculum | The Good and the Beautiful, literature/project curricula | Parent-led books, print work, projects | Complement or automate the planning/feedback burden rather than dismissing trusted curricula |
| Supplemental school systems | i-Ready, Exact Path, IXL | Diagnostics, practice, intervention, standards reports | Produce evidence during learning and include work outside the platform |
| LMS platforms | Canvas, Schoology, Google Classroom, Moodle | Course delivery, assignments, gradebook, identity, integrations | Primer is also an LMS, differentiated by native tutoring and mastery; enter schools supplementarily and integrate before asking buyers to consolidate platforms |
| High-touch alternative schools | Alpha School/2 Hour Learning, microschools | A whole-school model around personalized learning | Offer the instructional system without the school tuition or screen-maximalist model |

## Direct and adjacent competitors

### Khanmigo

**Strengths:** trusted Khan Academy brand, huge content library, Socratic tutoring, district distribution, and very low family pricing.

**Gap Primer targets:** a parent-directed longitudinal program, standards evidence attached to broader work, projects, cross-subject habit enforcement, and off-screen activity.

**Implication:** “better AI tutor” is not enough. Khanmigo anchors the commodity price of conversational tutoring.

### Synthesis Tutor

**Strengths:** polished conceptual mathematics tutoring, strong homeschool awareness, and family-friendly pricing.

**Gap Primer targets:** Synthesis is mathematics-focused and strongest in younger grades. Primer begins where its age range tapers and extends across subjects and projects.

**Implication:** Synthesis is evidence that families will adopt an AI tutor, but it also raises the product-quality bar for mathematics.

### Math Academy and ALEKS

**Strengths:** deep knowledge graphs, adaptive mastery, established mathematics credibility, clear progress records, and validated willingness to pay.

**Gap Primer targets:** single-subject scope, screen-based exercise, limited project evidence, and no cross-subject quality policy.

**Implication:** Primer's mathematics must be independently credible. Breadth cannot excuse a weak mastery model.

### Alpha School, 2 Hour Learning, and TimeBack

**Strengths:** the closest strategic thesis: mastery-based personalized academics, adults as guides, and time freed for broader activity.

**Gap Primer targets:** very high price, contested outcome claims, and an academic model still centered heavily on software time. Primer treats projects as standards-bearing instruction rather than an unrelated afternoon.

**Implication:** position against the model, not personalities: adult-directed, mostly off-screen, transparent evidence, and accessible to a household without buying a private school.

### Time4Learning, Power Homeschool, Miacademy, and Acellus

**Strengths:** broad curriculum, known homeschool workflows, automatic grading, transcripts/accreditation in some products, and predictable subscription pricing.

**Gap Primer targets:** lesson completion is not diagnosis; quiz scores are not a rich evidence record; adaptivity and feedback are limited; most learning remains on-platform.

**Implication:** these products define what “complete and easy” means. Primer needs comparable operational completeness, not just better pedagogy.

### The Good and the Beautiful and project/literature curricula

**Strengths:** trusted, inexpensive, parent-legible, and genuinely off-screen.

**Gap Primer targets:** heavy parent planning, teaching, adaptation, checking, and record-keeping burden.

**Implication:** these may be content partners or compatible inputs. Primer's claim is not that physical curriculum is obsolete; it is that the adult should not have to supply every repetitive tutoring and assessment action.

### i-Ready, Exact Path, MATHia, IXL, and Amira

**Strengths:** school procurement familiarity, diagnostics, standards reports, integration, implementation support, and in some cases strong efficacy evidence.

**Gap Primer targets:** subject silos, institution-only availability, screen-native evidence, and separate assessment cycles. Primer aims to capture evidence from normal tutoring, writing, and projects.

**Implication:** these are the benchmark for school evidence and reporting. Primer cannot enter schools on vision alone.

## Is Canvas a competitor?

Yes, at the LMS category level, but not yet as a direct substitute for Primer's instructional model. Canvas centers courses, assignments, content, grading, identity, analytics, and integrations. Primer will provide LMS functions while centering native tutoring, mastery progression, cross-subject habits, projects, and evidence. Mastery Connect and Instructure's AI study capabilities move Canvas closer to Primer's territory.

For Primer's initial school strategy, Canvas is:

1. an incumbent LMS Primer can coexist with during supplementary adoption;
2. an LTI launch and grade/evidence export target;
3. a distribution and workflow surface;
4. a direct long-term LMS competitor if Primer expands into primary institutional deployment;
5. a near-term instructional threat if its AI study coach becomes a longitudinal K–12 mastery tutor.

The pitch should say:

> Canvas can remain the school's system of record while Primer runs the supplementary programme. Over time, Primer can earn the right to own more of the LMS workflow because instruction and mastery are native to it.

## Primer's white space

No reviewed competitor clearly combines all of these:

- an adult, not the student, directs the system;
- specialist tutoring across middle-school core subjects;
- common standards for reasoning, grammar, evidence, units, and vocabulary across subjects;
- progression gated by standards-level evidence;
- deliberate transfer of skills into other subjects and tools;
- real projects treated as core instruction;
- substantial off-screen work;
- a portfolio that shows the artifact behind mastery;
- a household-first product that can later integrate with schools.

The moat is the integrated operating model and longitudinal evidence, not any one feature. Competitors can copy individual features quickly.

## Competitive risks

### Model commoditization

General AI products can provide good explanations for free. Primer must prove that governance, sequencing, evidence, and habit formation produce better outcomes and save adult time.

### Bundling

Google, Microsoft, Instructure, and existing curriculum vendors can bundle AI into products schools or families already use. Primer needs compatibility and a clearly superior instructional loop.

### Trust and distribution

Homeschool curricula have long-standing communities and worldview alignment. A new AI system begins with a trust deficit.

### Evidence disadvantage

School incumbents may have randomized studies, ESSA evidence, established assessments, and years of implementation data. Primer must make evaluation a core product discipline.

### Name collision

Primer (`primer.com`) is already a funded K–8 microschool company with overlapping customers and positioning. This creates brand confusion and potential legal risk. Complete trademark and naming diligence before treating “Primer” as a durable commercial asset.

## Positioning statement

> Primer is the adult-directed tutoring and mastery system for middle school. It teaches through specialist tutors, insists on reasoning and evidence in every subject, brings skills back in real projects, and gives the adult the work behind every mastery claim.

This is more defensible than “an AI LMS” and more specific than “personalized learning.”
