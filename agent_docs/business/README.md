# Primer business case workspace

This directory develops the business case behind Primer. It separates product claims, market facts, modeled assumptions, strategic choices, and unanswered diligence questions so the pitch can become sharper without overstating what has been proved.

## Working thesis

Primer is an LMS built around instruction rather than course administration alone. It gives an adult the reach of a disciplined one-to-one tutor: specialist instruction, immediate correction, standards-level evidence, and deliberate transfer of skills into real work, with low educator burden. Homeschool families remain the clearest beachhead, while remediation purchased by parents of public- and private-school students creates an earlier path into both sectors without requiring a school procurement decision. Co-ops, microschools, and direct school pilots expand the same learning record into institutional use.

## Current strategic answers

1. **Core beachhead:** homeschool families in grades 5–8, especially families already combining curriculum, tutors, projects, and state reporting.
2. **Parallel family market:** parents use Primer after school or alongside school to close gaps, extend learning, and maintain a parent-visible mastery record.
3. **School entry:** supplementary family use can establish demand and outcomes inside public and private school communities before institutional adoption. School pilots can begin with bounded tutoring, intervention, enrichment, or project-learning use cases.
4. **Category:** Primer is an LMS whose defining capability is conducting instruction and maintaining the evidence behind mastery, not merely distributing content and collecting assignments. It can coexist and integrate with incumbent LMS, SIS, and identity systems.
5. **Proof burden:** the research supports intensive tutoring and formative feedback in general. It does not yet prove that Primer or an LLM tutor reproduces human-tutoring effects. Pilot outcomes must become the central evidence.

## Open decisions

- Exact first paid scope inside curriculum planning, assessment evaluation, and standards/mastery records
- Validate Base at $50 per learner/month, then Core at $100 and Premier at $300 against differentiated scope, COGS, and retention
- Initial geography: Tennessee alignment versus an ESA-first state
- Whether the brand can coexist with the established `primer.com` microschool company
- Validate the working $3.5 million, 18-month pre-seed against actual hiring, inference, and pilot plans
- Validate the seed target case: 1,500 paying learners, $900K ARR run rate, 85%+ 12-week retention, and 70%+ post-credit gross margin
- Independent education/research advisors and the first experienced educator product owner
- Pilot design, outcome measure, control/comparison strategy, and timeline
- Capital-efficient proof plan and next-round milestones

## Research discipline

- Prefer government data, peer-reviewed research, official product pages, and public contracts.
- Build market size from customer units multiplied by plausible annual revenue, not broad education-spending reports.
- Label every number as observed, estimated, or modeled.
- Do not use Bloom's “two sigma” as a promised outcome.
- Do not present architecture or planned features as demonstrated efficacy.
- Date all market figures because homeschool definitions and education-choice programs change quickly.
