# Review of the initial Primer pitch deck

Reviewed source: `/home/aleks/work/primer-pitch-site/source/Primer Pitch Deck.dc.html`.

## What the deck already does well

The deck has a clear product conviction: “A student moves on when they can explain the step, not when the week ends.” It explains the product better than most early education decks because it shows mechanisms rather than relying on “personalized AI” language.

Its strongest sequence is:

1. adult direction;
2. specialist tutors;
3. habit checks before content evaluation;
4. cross-subject reinforcement;
5. a concrete tutor thread;
6. continuous, periodic, and portfolio evidence;
7. real projects tied to standards.

The tutor-thread example is especially effective. It shows that Primer refuses an unsupported answer, locates a misconception without simply supplying the correction, catches a unit error, and connects mathematics to a real project.

## The central problem

The deck is a strong product explainer, not yet a business or investment case. It ends after the instructional model and omits the questions a buyer or investor will ask next:

- Who buys first, and why now?
- How many buyers exist and what do they already spend?
- What is the initial product and price?
- How will Primer reach customers economically?
- What evidence supports the instructional thesis?
- What evidence is specific to Primer?
- Why will this team win?
- What is the competitive category?
- Can the same product enter schools, and what must change first?
- What milestones does capital buy?

## Claims that need tightening

### The classroom problem is illustrated, not evidenced

“Twenty-eight students, one pace” and the growing gap chart communicate the intuition, but the chart is unlabeled as an illustration and has no empirical source. Either mark it explicitly as conceptual or replace it with sourced data. Do not imply that all classrooms literally operate at one pace.

### “How you know it worked” currently means measurement, not efficacy

The evidence section explains what Primer records. It does not show that Primer improves outcomes. Current family use began recently and is limited to command-teacher and PrimerTV; subject tutoring, projects, and assessment remain planned. Rename this section to “How Primer will know what the student knows” until pilot data exists. A later section should distinguish:

- evidence for the underlying methods;
- evidence generated about each student's work;
- evidence that Primer itself improves learning.

### Some absolutes are too strong

“Nothing advances on time elapsed,” “the wrong step is questioned, never corrected,” and “zero weeks of test cramming” are memorable but brittle. Good tutors sometimes model a correction, direct instruction sometimes outperforms unguided questioning, and the assessment plan itself contains a six-week spring intensive. Use operational language rather than absolutes.

Suggested replacements:

- “Advancement is gated by demonstrated mastery, not seat time alone.”
- “Primer asks the student to locate and repair the error before showing the correction.”
- “Test format is practiced throughout the year, reducing the need for last-minute cramming.”

### The two audiences need one adoption story

The deck speaks to homeschool parents and classroom teachers but does not explain how the markets connect. State the sequence clearly: homeschool families establish the full-curriculum use case; parents of public- and private-school students use Primer as a supplementary LMS for gap-closing and extension; demonstrated family demand and outcomes create an earlier path to bounded school pilots; institutional adoption adds rostering, governance, and interoperability.

### Grade range is inconsistent

The deck says grades 5–8 while project documentation and standards examples emphasize grades 6–8. Choose a launch range and distinguish launch scope from eventual scope.

### Define what kind of LMS Primer is

Primer will be an LMS, but it should not be framed as a conventional LMS with AI added. Canvas, Schoology, Moodle, and Google Classroom center course delivery, assignments, identity, and grading. Primer's LMS centers the instructional loop and the evidence behind mastery while also owning curriculum, assignments, portfolios, assessment, and records. In schools it can operate as the supplementary LMS for a bounded program or integrate with an incumbent LMS rather than requiring immediate replacement.

### Brand collision needs diligence

`primer.com` is already an established K–8 microschool company serving overlapping families and using overlapping language around fundamentals, mastery, and projects. This creates likely search, positioning, and possible trademark risk. Resolve naming before scaling marketing spend.

## Missing business section

Add a fourth section after the product and evidence story.

### Recommended new slides

1. **Why now**
   - homeschool enrollment remains above pre-pandemic levels;
   - education-choice funding is expanding;
   - capable language models make responsive tutoring economically possible;
   - parents and schools are looking for accountable use of AI, not generic chat.

2. **The first customer**
   - intentional homeschool families with students in grades 5–8;
   - current substitute is a stack of curriculum, parent teaching, online practice, and occasional tutoring;
   - core job: help the adult know what to teach, hold standards consistently, and prove what was learned.

3. **Market, bottom up**
   - roughly 3.0–3.75 million U.S. homeschool students;
   - modeled 1.5–1.9 million households;
   - show TAM at several explicit annual revenue assumptions;
   - show a narrower initial SAM rather than declaring all homeschool spending addressable.

4. **Go to market**
   - founder-led design partners;
   - parent referrals and case studies;
   - co-op/microschool leaders as aggregators;
   - selected ESA marketplace approval;
   - private-school pilots after household retention and outcomes are established.

5. **Expansion path**
   - homeschool family → supplementary use by school families → co-op/microschool and bounded school pilots → broader institutional adoption;
   - show that direct parent adoption and school adoption can proceed in parallel;
   - list the product capabilities required at each transition.

6. **Evidence base and proof plan**
   - peer-reviewed tutoring pooled effect around 0.29 SD;
   - acknowledge scale and modality caveats;
   - define the Primer pilot outcome and pre-specified success threshold.

7. **Competition**
   - generic AI/study modes;
   - direct AI tutors;
   - homeschool curriculum platforms;
   - mastery systems;
   - LMS/infrastructure;
   - state why Primer's combination is distinct.

8. **Why Aleks Clark**
   - homeschooled in grades K–7, 9, and 10 while living in Peru;
   - used Abeka across all subjects to homeschool two daughters for three years through grades 5–8;
   - has begun using command-teacher and PrimerTV with his sixth-grade son;
   - has experienced the strengths and limitations of homeschooling as both student and parent;
   - spent five years building eBackpack for more than half a million students, including direct SIS, SSO, grade/roster, assessment, standards, and content integration work;
   - domain gaps and hires/advisors remain explicit.

9. **Business model and economics**
   - working hypothesis: about $50 per learner per month plus included usage and metered overage;
   - expected model-compute and support costs;
   - gross-margin target after real usage data;
   - premium add-ons such as PrimerTV, supervised workstations, and bring-your-own-content hosting;
   - family versus institutional pricing;
   - ESA funding is upside, not required for the initial model.

10. **Current state**
    - approximately 40 founder hours and $1,000 invested;
    - command-teacher, PrimerTV, basic LMS core, and the Ultralogical agent platform are demonstrable;
    - one week of initial family use is product discovery, not traction;
    - no learning-outcome or retention claims yet.

11. **Ask and milestones**
    - working target: $3.5 million for 18 months;
    - founder plus two high-leverage hires at $300,000 annual cash salary with strong benefits and a smaller option footprint;
    - full paid-remediation loop, core tutoring/LMS/standards assessment capability, and independently measured learning evidence;
    - instrumented inference economics and a viable usage policy at the working family price;
    - LTI and Clever school-pilot readiness after family proof;
    - customer, revenue, learning-outcome, use-of-funds, and next-round milestones must be quantified before external fundraising.

## Recommended narrative

The sharper story is not “AI can teach every child.” It is:

> Families and teachers know that a student needs immediate, patient correction and must demonstrate mastery before moving on. Human one-to-one tutoring works, but it is expensive and difficult to provide across every subject. Primer turns the adult's standards into a persistent instructional system: it tutors, insists on reasoning and evidence, brings skills back in new contexts, and records the work behind every mastery claim.

That claim is differentiated, testable, and does not depend on promising human-tutor equivalence before it has been demonstrated.
