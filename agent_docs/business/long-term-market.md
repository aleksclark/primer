# Long-term market and pricing expansion

## Thesis

Primer's initial $50 product proves the instructional engine through a focused two- or three-subject tutor. The long-term opportunity comes from expanding the same longitudinal learner record into broader, higher-value education packages:

1. **Base tutor:** selected subjects and standards evidence;
2. **Core education:** full-spectrum academic coverage comparable to a public-school core programme;
3. **Premier education:** deeply customized, multi-domain tutoring and planning for private-school, private-tutor, and high-intent families;
4. **Institutional deployments:** targeted intervention, learning support, microschools, elite schools, and international schools.

The expansion is not simply charging more for the same chatbot. Each tier owns more curriculum breadth, planning, assessment, content, projects, family reporting, and human/service accountability.

## Consumer package ladder

### Base tutor — $50/month/learner

**Annual revenue:** $600

**Product scope:**

- two or three selected subjects;
- adaptive tutoring and practice;
- state-standards mapping;
- assessment and mastery record;
- parent progress view;
- defined included inference allowance;
- projects and content limited to the selected subjects.

**Primary buyers:**

- homeschool families assembling an à-la-carte stack;
- public/private school families remediating one or more subjects;
- advanced students seeking deeper work;
- ESA families buying a focused service.

**Competitive frame:** full-month adaptive attention for less than one hour of many human tutoring services.

### Core education — $100/month/learner

**Annual revenue:** $1,200

**Product scope:**

- full core academics: mathematics, ELA, science, and social studies;
- cross-subject habits and reinforcement;
- curriculum and weekly planning;
- assessments, mastery records, and portfolio;
- syllabus/content ingestion;
- parent-directed projects;
- school-grade and state-assessment remediation views;
- broader included usage.

**Primary buyers:**

- homeschool families seeking a complete academic spine;
- public-school families wanting a comprehensive supplement;
- microschools and hybrid programmes with lean instructional staffing;
- funded tutoring/microgrant families.

**Competitive frame:** $1,200 per year is within the $1,200–$2,500 per-student range Stanford's National Student Support Accelerator advises districts to expect for high-impact tutoring, while remaining far below full online/private school tuition.

**School-side caution:** districts will not buy this as a $1,200 software license for every student. The institutional offer must be a targeted tutoring/intervention service for a defined cohort, funded and evaluated as instructional capacity.

### Premier education — $300/month/learner

**Annual revenue:** $3,600

**Product scope:**

- everything in Core;
- a fully customized scope and sequence across academic and practical domains;
- school-specific pedagogy and content guardrails;
- advanced courses, test preparation, executive-function support, and long-term planning;
- higher-touch parent/educator reporting;
- bring-your-own-content ingestion and managed library;
- PrimerTV/media curation and project orchestration;
- larger usage envelope and priority support;
- optional scheduled human expert review or specialist marketplace access;
- premium institutional integrations and branded experiences.

**Primary buyers:**

- families currently stacking human tutors, test prep, executive-function coaching, and educational consulting;
- elite independent and boarding-school families;
- disability-funded families needing coordinated learning support;
- concierge homeschool families;
- elite schools embedding a customized tutoring programme into tuition or offering it as a learning-support service.

**Competitive frame:** $300 buys roughly 4–8 hours of marketplace tutoring, about four hours in a managed tutoring membership, fewer than two hours of premium test prep, or less than one hour of elite educational consulting. Primer offers persistent coverage across the month and a unified learner record.

## Price-to-value evidence

- Care.com and Wyzant place ordinary tutoring broadly around $25–$60 per hour.
- Varsity Tutors has offered a roughly $299 monthly membership with four tutoring hours and classes.
- Princeton Review publishes test-prep tutoring around $175 per hour and higher for premium packages.
- Independent educational consultants commonly charge about $140–$200 per hour, with family packages of several thousand dollars.
- Executive-function and learning specialists commonly charge more than $100 per session.
- EdChoice/Morning Consult research reports average stated parent willingness to pay around **$357 per month** for tutoring, with interest above 55% among private-school and special-education families.

The Premier tier is positioned at the top of mainstream managed tutoring, not at the ultra-high-net-worth concierge ceiling. Its value must come from breadth, persistence, customization, evidence, and coordination rather than human hours alone.

## Premium private-school opportunity

### Market units

NAIS 2024–25 reporting data covers:

- **1,331 schools**;
- **631,672 learners**;
- average day tuition of **$32,251**;
- average seven-day boarding tuition of **$71,715**;
- median total school expense of **$34,717 per learner**;
- median student-to-teacher ratio of **8.2:1**.

NCES 2020–21 tuition-band data identifies:

- **1,159,670 private-school learners** across **4,580 schools** charging at least $15,000 in tuition;
- **600,680 nonsectarian learners** across **2,870 schools** charging at least $15,000;
- average tuition of **$34,610** for the nonsectarian premium cohort.

The NCES tuition data is old, uses sticker price rather than net tuition, and requires inflation/vintage caveats. It remains the best federal bottom-up premium segmentation table currently available.

### Revenue ceilings at $300/month

| Premium cohort | Learners | Annual revenue per learner | Modeled ceiling |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| NAIS DASL reporting universe | 631,672 | $3,600 | **$2.27B** |
| Nonsectarian schools at $15K+ tuition | 600,680 | $3,600 | **$2.16B** |
| All private schools at $15K+ tuition | 1,159,670 | $3,600 | **$4.17B** |

These cohorts overlap and must not be added. They describe alternative boundaries for the premium opportunity.

### How elite schools buy

A school cannot normally absorb $3,600 per learner from its ordinary technology budget. NAIS medians are approximately $524 per learner for technology and $1,167 for instructional expenses outside core compensation. The Premier offer therefore enters through one of three motions:

1. **Parent-paid school community benefit:** the school endorses or integrates Primer while families subscribe directly.
2. **Targeted learning-support programme:** the school buys Premier for a smaller group of learners as a substitute for tutoring, study-hall staffing, executive-function coaching, or specialist support.
3. **Tuition-embedded academic programme:** Primer becomes part of the school's instructional model and is priced against tuition, teaching capacity, retention, and differentiation rather than the software budget.

At average NAIS day tuition, $3,600 is about 11% of sticker tuition. That is too high for an undifferentiated school-wide add-on but credible for a targeted service, parent-paid programme, or meaningful replacement of existing tutoring and instructional expense. At average boarding tuition it is about 5%.

### Elite-school product requirements

- school-authored instructional guardrails and pedagogy;
- Harkness, AP, IB, writing-intensive, classical, faith, or other configurable methods;
- Blackbaud, Veracross, Ravenna, LTI, OneRoster, and identity integration;
- branded parent and student experiences;
- teacher-visible transcripts and intervention controls;
- academic-integrity modes;
- learning-support and accommodation workflows;
- high-quality writing and oral-assessment evaluation;
- premium content ingestion and rights management;
- independent efficacy evidence;
- security, accessibility, privacy, and audit packages;
- low-friction rollout that does not initially require SIS/LMS replacement.

### Boarding-school wedge

Boarding schools are a concentrated, high-value reference market. Primer can address:

- evening study hall and dorm academic support;
- international/EAL learner scaffolding;
- asynchronous help across time zones;
- guardian reporting across languages;
- consistent support without adding a specialist for every subject and dorm.

The boarding pool is small, roughly 200+ TABS member schools, and recent enrollment pressure is a risk. Its strategic value is premium references, concentrated distribution, and a clear staffing substitute.

## Public-school-equivalent opportunity

### Family-paid

NCES reports approximately **14.61 million public learners in grades 5–8** in fall 2024. At $100 per month, the theoretical full-population ceiling is:

`14.61M learners × $1,200/year = $17.53B`

This is not a serviceable market estimate. It illustrates the large population available for parent-paid comprehensive supplementation. The SAM must filter for families actively seeking tutoring, willingness to pay, subject need, and reachable channels.

### School-paid

The same $1,200 annual price fits the low end of high-impact tutoring budgets, not software budgets. School deployments should target 5–15% of enrollment for intervention, mandated remediation, summer learning, or learning support.

A useful district equation is:

`targeted learners × $1,200 annual service price`

Example: a 5,000-student district serving 10% of learners produces a $600,000 annual programme, subject to procurement, implementation, and outcomes requirements.

The offer must report dosage, active use, standards progress, and cost per successful learner. Outcomes-based contracting may place 10–50% of payment at risk in some programmes.

## Other high-value expansion markets

### Specialized learning support

Students with dyslexia, ADHD, executive-function needs, or other learning differences already compare against specialists charging $90–$265 per hour and private specialized schools costing tens of thousands annually. Disability-weighted ESAs can exceed $40,000 in some cases.

A $300 Premier tier is compelling if Primer can support accommodations, IEP/504 goals, structured progress monitoring, service logs, expert review, and safe escalation. This market has high willingness to pay and high evidence/liability requirements.

### Microschools and hybrid academies

The National Microschooling Center reports median private microschool enrollment around 20 learners and median tuition around $6,500. Full-program platform precedents range from roughly $2,000 to $6,500 per learner for school-of-record models.

Primer can price above the consumer Core tier when it becomes the school's academic operating system, but the national site count is not reliable. Size this market from approved ESA-provider lists and reachable associations, not the frequently repeated 95,000-site estimate.

### International schools

ISC Research tracks approximately **15,075 international schools**, **7.6 million learners**, and **$69.3 billion in fee income** as of 2025. Institutional pricing of $50–$300 per learner annually creates a broad expansion pool, with higher tiers in Gulf, Hong Kong, Singapore, and premium global school groups.

Requirements include IB/IGCSE/A-level/AP curricula, multilingual support, regional data residency, GDPR and EU AI Act readiness, safeguarding, and group-level multi-country administration.

### Education-choice programmes

More than one million learners participate in private-choice programmes, and ESAs commonly cover tutoring, online programmes, therapies, and instructional materials. The federal Education Freedom Tax Credit begins in 2027 in participating states.

Choice funding makes $1,200 and $3,600 annual packages accessible beyond high-income households. It is a distribution and affordability channel, not a separate learner population to add to private/homeschool TAM without deduplication.

## Non-overlapping opportunity map

| Opportunity | Buyer | Working annual ARPU | Addressable unit | Strategic role |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Base family tutor | Parent | $600 | Focused homeschool/supplement learner | Initial acquisition and product proof |
| Core family education | Parent/ESA | $1,200 | Full-spectrum homeschool/supplement learner | ARPU expansion and curriculum breadth |
| Premier family education | Parent/ESA | $3,600 | High-intent, private-school, specialist-support learner | Premium margin and long LTV |
| Targeted public/private intervention | School | ~$1,200 | Supported learner, not whole enrollment | Institutional outcomes and scale |
| Elite learning support | School/family | $1,000–$3,600+ | Supported learner | Premium references and staffing substitution |
| Microschool academic core | School founder | $1,500–$6,500 | Enrolled learner | Highest institutional ownership of workflow |
| International school platform | School/group | $50–$300 | All enrolled learners | Large global school-side volume |
| Diocesan/system deployment | School system | $25–$60 | All enrolled learners | Concentrated low-ARPU distribution |

The rows are alternative contracts. A learner should be counted once under the party paying Primer.

## Expansion sequence

### Phase 1: prove the engine

- Base tutor at $50;
- homeschool and parent-paid remediation;
- standards progression, retention, inference economics;
- conversion tests into Core.

### Phase 2: expand share of wallet

- Core at $100;
- full core curriculum, projects, portfolio, and syllabus ingestion;
- ESA approval and microschool pilots;
- targeted intervention programme for schools.

### Phase 3: establish premium value

- Premier at $300;
- private-school families, specialist support, test prep, executive function, managed content;
- elite day and boarding-school learning-support pilots;
- optional expert-review network.

### Phase 4: institutional and global scale

- tuition-embedded elite-school programmes;
- microschool/school-of-record academic core;
- diocesan and network contracts;
- international curriculum and data-residency expansion;
- public district expansion only where tutoring/intervention funding and efficacy evidence support $1,200 per learner.

## Investor framing

The long-term story is a vertical expansion in responsibility and ARPU:

> Primer enters at $50 as a focused tutor, expands to $100 as the learner's full academic spine, and reaches $300 where it coordinates the customized education stack that families and elite schools currently assemble from tutors, coaches, curriculum, assessment, and consulting. Institutional contracts extend the same engine from one learner to whole programmes without pricing a tutoring service like commodity LMS software.

Do not add every modeled ceiling into one TAM. Present the $2.04B homeschool Base ceiling, $17.53B public-grade family Core population ceiling, and $2.27B–$4.17B premium private Premier ceilings as distinct expansion layers with explicit overlap.
