## Primary-page sequence

### 1. Header

- Primer mark and wordmark
- anchored navigation
- `Product state` legend
- `Discuss the round` button

Behavior: transparent at page top, compact persistent bar after first scroll.

### 2. Hero: the standard

- single proposition
- one-line company definition
- primary CTA: discuss the round
- secondary CTA: view the product
- subtle proof label: `Instructional LMS · Grades 5–8 · Family first, school ready`

Avoid product screenshots in the first viewport. Establish the conviction first.

### 3. Problem: attention does not scale

Two synchronized columns:

- family curriculum package: rigorous but static, parent carries enforcement and grading;
- classroom: teacher cannot respond continuously to every learner.

A center rail shows the shared missing actions: diagnose, correct, adapt, revisit, record.

Use founder experience as a concrete case, not as proof of the whole market.

### 4. Product: an LMS that teaches

Interactive system diagram:

```text
Adult direction
      ↓
Curriculum planner → Specialist tutor → Habit checks
      ↓                    ↓                 ↓
Standards map ← Evidence record ← Student revision
      ↓
Next task / transfer / project
```

Toggling a node reveals a short explanation and the current product-state badge.

### 5. Product demonstration

Show one truthful end-to-end remediation scenario:

1. parent pastes syllabus or states the target;
2. Primer identifies prerequisite standards;
3. student attempts a task;
4. tutor asks for reasoning;
5. habit guard catches a missing unit or unsupported claim;
6. student revises;
7. Primer records evidence and schedules a later transfer check.

Until this works in the product, label the flow `TARGET EXPERIENCE` and separately show live command-teacher/PrimerTV artifacts.

### 6. How it teaches

Horizontal or pinned-scroll sequence:

- specialist attention;
- correction before progression;
- habits across subjects;
- transfer into projects;
- evidence behind mastery;
- adult authority.

Each panel uses one concrete example and no more than two sentences.

### 7. Evidence

Three tiers visually separated:

1. **Research basis:** tutoring meta-analysis, mastery/adaptive evidence, caveats.
2. **Learner evidence:** what Primer records per standard.
3. **Company efficacy:** currently unproven, followed by the pilot and external-assessment plan.

The third card must not be visually implied to contain results.

### 8. Market

Use an interactive calculator with explicit fields:

- segment;
- learners;
- annual revenue per learner;
- serviceable filters;
- adoption assumption.

Default view:

- 3.4 million modeled homeschool learners;
- $600 annual working price;
- $2.04 billion modeled revenue ceiling;
- bright warning: `TAM scenario, not forecast or SAM`.

Use an expansion-ladder view rather than only segment tabs:

- Base homeschool: $2.04B modeled ceiling;
- Core public grades 5–8 family population: $17.53B theoretical ceiling;
- Premier NAIS: $2.27B modeled ceiling;
- Premier $15K+ private tuition cohort: $4.17B modeled ceiling.

Prevent overlap from being summed automatically. A secondary tab view can break out families, public intervention, elite private, microschool, and international opportunities.

### 9. Go to market

An overlapping adoption ladder rather than a rigid timeline:

- family homeschool;
- family remediation alongside school;
- co-op/microschool;
- one-student or bounded school pilot;
- LTI + Clever supplementary deployment;
- roster/grade integration;
- de facto primary LMS.

Each rung shows buyer, product scope, proof required, and sales friction.

### 10. Competition

Use category columns instead of a logo graveyard:

- generic AI;
- AI tutor;
- mastery subject tool;
- homeschool package;
- conventional LMS;
- Primer.

Rows:

- adult-directed;
- longitudinal curriculum planning;
- cross-subject habits;
- off-screen projects;
- evidence per standard;
- family purchase;
- institutional integrations.

Avoid universal checkmarks unless verified. Link methodology.

### 11. Founder

Use a three-part timeline:

- homeschool student in Peru;
- homeschool parent using Abeka;
- LMS/platform engineer building Primer.

Technical proof strip:

- >500,000 eBackpack students;
- ~60,000 requests/minute;
- 99.9% uptime;
- Skyward, PowerSchool, Google Classroom, OneDrive, OAuth, Common Cartridge.

Adjacent candid team card:

- solo founder today;
- 18-month target: founder plus one or two high-leverage hires;
- $300,000 annual cash salary per team member plus strong benefits;
- first hire: experienced educator/product owner;
- second hire: experienced product/platform builder;
- smaller option grants are an intentional cash/equity trade, not a claim of lower total dilution.

### 12. Current state

A release board with three columns:

**Live**
- command-teacher
- PrimerTV
- LMS core
- Ultralogical agent platform

**In development**
- select only work actively underway

**Planned**
- subject tutors
- projects
- standards assessment
- mobile
- LTI/Clever/SIS
- internationalization

Below it:

- ~40 founder hours
- ~$1,000 invested
- one week of initial family use
- no retention, revenue, or efficacy claims

### 13. Business model

Show a three-step responsibility and ARPU ladder:

- **Base, $50/learner/month:** two or three subjects;
- **Core, $100/learner/month:** full core academics and planning;
- **Premier, $300/learner/month:** customized full-spectrum education, managed content, advanced support, and higher-touch reporting.

For each tier show added capability, included inference allowance, target COGS, onboarding model, and intended buyer. Add a separate institutional row: targeted public intervention around $1,200/supported learner/year and premium-school programmes priced against learning-support or academic-core value.

A unit-economics panel stays blank or marked `MEASURING` until actual usage exists.

### 14. Round

Headline: `$3.5M pre-seed · 18 months · working target`

Show the bottom-up base case:

- three $300,000 annual cash salaries;
- 30% employment load for strong benefits and payroll overhead;
- inference, infrastructure, hardware, research, compliance, and pilot costs modeled separately;
- 20% contingency;
- 5–8% option-pool scenario tested against actual hiring commitments.

Use-of-funds groups:

- paid remediation and LMS product;
- curriculum/assessment and educator leadership;
- research and learning validation;
- inference economics and production reliability;
- school interoperability/compliance;
- customer discovery and pilot operations.

The visual must distinguish option-pool dilution from financing dilution. Show the month-18 seed-readiness scorecard directly below the budget:

- 1,500 paying learners, with 1,000 as the floor;
- $900K ARR run rate, with $600K as the floor;
- 85%+ 12-week paid retention, with 75% as the floor;
- 70%+ post-credit gross margin, with 60% as the floor;
- ≤$15 monthly COGS per learner and ≤5-month CAC payback;
- independent standards progression and ≥90% expert agreement on mastery scoring;
- one to three bounded paid school pilots.

Label these as targets purchased by the pre-seed, not current traction.

### 15. Close/contact

Restate the standard and show:

- founder name;
- location;
- email;
- meeting CTA;
- links to evidence, market model, and diligence.

Do not include a generic “thank you” section.

## Responsive behavior

- Desktop: pinned narratives, diagrams, and side-by-side evidence.
- Tablet: preserve section order and diagrams without horizontal overflow.
- Mobile: all interactive visuals receive an equivalent linear reading order.
- Motion must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Charts and diagrams require accessible text summaries.

## Analytics events

Track only investor-intent and content-quality events:

- CTA click by section;
- demo start/completion;
- evidence deep-dive open;
- market assumptions changed;
- diligence page viewed;
- contact submitted.

Do not track sensitive student or family information on the investor site.
