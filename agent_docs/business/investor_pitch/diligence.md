# Investor diligence structure

The primary site makes the argument. Diligence pages show the work, unresolved assumptions, and evidence needed to underwrite it.

## Public diligence

### Product state

For every capability list:

- status: live, in development, or planned;
- demonstrable artifact;
- owner;
- target milestone;
- key dependency;
- known failure mode.

Initial inventory:

| Capability | Current status | Current evidence |
|---|---|---|
| command-teacher | Live | Linux command tutor demonstration |
| PrimerTV | Live/early use | Running TV/content system |
| LMS core | Live foundation | APIs, admin SPA, standards/mastery models |
| Ultralogical | Live foundation | Agent execution platform |
| Core subject tutors | Planned | Architecture and teaching specification |
| Cross-subject guards | Planned | Injector design |
| Project orchestration | Planned | Project framework |
| Autonomous mastery assessment | Planned | Assessment model and evidence plan |
| Mobile application | Planned | Financing milestone |
| LTI/Clever | Planned | School-entry strategy |
| Broader SIS/SSO | Planned | Founder experience and roadmap |
| Internationalization | Planned | Financing milestone |

### Market model

Expose:

- all population sources and vintages;
- homeschool definition range;
- household derivation;
- grade-band assumptions;
- willingness-to-pay assumptions;
- family/school overlap handling;
- annual price sensitivity;
- initial SAM filters once researched;
- SOM acquisition and retention assumptions once observed.

Never expose one blended market number without equations.

### Research basis

Provide:

- primary paper links;
- effect size and population;
- outcome type;
- implementation requirements;
- scale/modality caveats;
- exact claim Primer uses;
- claims Primer rejects.

### Proof plan

Required before external fundraising:

- launch subject and standards;
- independent assessment instrument;
- baseline and follow-up cadence;
- primary outcome and failure threshold;
- dosage target;
- comparison condition;
- handling of attrition;
- mastery-scoring validation;
- anomaly/cheating checks;
- independent reviewer;
- privacy/consent protocol;
- publication policy for null or adverse findings.

### School readiness

Roadmap by gate:

1. family privacy and COPPA programme;
2. LTI launch and Clever SSO;
3. organizational roles and transcript review;
4. OneRoster/grade synchronization;
5. WCAG conformance and VPAT;
6. DPA/NDPA readiness;
7. SOC 2 Type II;
8. state-specific contracting;
9. broader SIS and identity adapters;
10. primary-LMS migration and administration.

### Competition methodology

For each competitor record:

- official product URL;
- target customer;
- current official price or `not public`;
- product scope;
- standards/mastery model;
- adult governance;
- project/off-screen support;
- family and school availability;
- interoperability;
- independent evidence;
- date verified.

## Gated diligence

### Financial model

Must include:

- family subscription assumptions;
- included inference allowance;
- model cost per active learner by usage percentile;
- support cost per account;
- media/storage cost;
- gross margin by plan;
- annual and monthly pricing sensitivity;
- churn/retention cohorts;
- institutional contract assumptions;
- hiring plan;
- 18-month cash plan;
- downside runway scenarios.

### Use of funds

The working $3.5 million pre-seed ask needs a role- and milestone-based model. The base case is founder plus two employees, each at $300,000 annual cash salary, approximately 30% employment load, separately modeled operating costs, and 20% contingency.

Minimum categories:

- founder compensation;
- experienced educator/product owner;
- experienced product/platform builder;
- contracted curriculum and assessment specialists;
- selected engineering and mobile work;
- learning research and external evaluation;
- safety, privacy, accessibility, and security;
- cloud/model/media infrastructure;
- design-partner support and customer research;
- school integrations and implementation;
- sales/marketing only after retention evidence;
- operating reserve.

### Architecture and security

- system architecture;
- model-provider strategy;
- student-data flows;
- subprocessor list;
- tenant isolation;
- encryption and key management;
- audit logging;
- retention/deletion implementation;
- incident response;
- safety policy and escalation;
- inference-cost controls;
- model evaluation and rollback.

### Founder references

- resume;
- employment verification where appropriate;
- references for eBackpack scale and integration work;
- references for Curri agent platform and cross-team leadership;
- product code/demo access;
- explanation of current employment and time commitment;
- IP assignment and conflicts review.

## Material risks

Present these directly:

1. **Very early product use:** one week is insufficient for retention or outcome evidence.
2. **Solo-founder concentration:** curriculum, safety, privacy, accessibility, and product judgment currently rest with Aleks.
3. **Founder-bias risk:** explicit concern about building the product he wants rather than one with broad demand.
4. **Autonomous assessment risk:** mastery evaluation may fail silently or be gamed.
5. **Inference economics:** usage variance may undermine the $50 price hypothesis.
6. **Consumer acquisition:** homeschool and parent channels are fragmented and trust-heavy.
7. **Institutional burden:** supplementary entry lowers but does not remove privacy, security, accessibility, and procurement requirements.
8. **Incumbent bundling:** generic AI and incumbent LMS platforms can add tutoring features.
9. **Evidence transfer:** human tutoring research does not establish LLM efficacy.
10. **Brand collision:** the existing Primer microschool company overlaps in name, customer, and positioning.
11. **Compact-team execution:** the plan concentrates responsibility in three senior roles. Hiring quality, role clarity, succession coverage, and milestone ownership must remain explicit.
12. **Data retention:** permanent LMS retention conflicts with data-minimization and institutional deletion expectations; policy must be revised before launch.
13. **Anonymized beta training:** any future training programme needs rigorous consent, deidentification, legal review, and no effect on core access.

## Open diligence questions

- What is the narrow launch subject and customer?
- What specific product can a family pay for within the next milestone?
- What does one or two-session time-to-value mean operationally?
- What is the maximum included model cost at $50/month?
- What percentage of the target market will accept metered education software?
- Who independently owns curriculum and research quality?
- Does the full three-person team need to start immediately, or should the second employee follow an activation/retention milestone?
- What focused equity grants align each high-leverage candidate with the long-term company outcome?
- What financing terms make the $3.5 million raise acceptable after option-pool dilution?
- Which milestones justify the next financing?
- How will internationalization create ESL value rather than only translated UI?
- What parts of Ultralogical are owned, open source, or encumbered?
- What founder time commitment begins after financing?
