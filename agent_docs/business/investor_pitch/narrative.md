# Investor narrative

## One-sentence company

Primer is an adult-directed instructional LMS that gives each learner persistent, standards-aligned tutoring and gives the adult evidence behind every mastery claim.

## The narrative arc

### 1. The standard

**A student should move on when they can explain and use the skill, not when the calendar advances.**

Open on the educational principle. Do not open on market size, founder biography, or a chat interface.

### 2. The constraint

Good parents and teachers know how to respond to one learner: diagnose the mistake, insist on a complete explanation, change the next task, and check again later. They cannot provide that attention continuously across every learner and subject.

The existing alternatives split the job:

- curriculum packages schedule content but do not adapt;
- generic AI answers questions but does not own the educational trajectory;
- subject tools optimize one domain;
- conventional LMS platforms distribute and collect work but do not conduct instruction;
- human tutoring works but is costly and narrow.

### 3. Founder insight

Aleks Clark experienced that constraint as both student and parent.

- Homeschooled in grades K–7, 9, and 10 while living in Peru.
- Benefited from self-direction, flexible pacing, reading, and learning by doing.
- Later used Abeka across all subjects to homeschool two daughters for three years through grades 5–8.
- Found it rigorous but inflexible, easy to game in assessment, and responsible for more than ten parent hours per week.
- Is now beginning to use command-teacher and PrimerTV with his sixth-grade son.

The sharp founder insight:

> The parent should set direction and values. The system should carry the repetitive burden of insisting on the standard.

### 4. Product thesis

Primer owns the long-run learning record because education is cumulative. It uses that record to plan, teach, assess, reinforce, and decide what comes next.

The instructional loop:

1. adult sets curriculum, worldview, content guardrails, and priorities;
2. planner chooses the next standards-linked task;
3. specialist tutor diagnoses and scaffolds;
4. habit guards require reasoning, evidence, units, grammar, and precise language;
5. student revises rather than receiving passive correction;
6. the skill reappears in another subject or project;
7. Primer stores the artifact behind mastery;
8. adult reviews exceptions and consequential decisions.

### 5. Why an LMS

A tutor conversation is episodic. Education is longitudinal.

Primer must own at launch:

- curriculum planning;
- assessment evaluation;
- standards and mastery records.

Over time it also owns assignments, projects, portfolios, content, communication, and institutional administration. The LMS is not a wrapper around the tutor; it is the memory and governance system that makes individual tutoring coherent over years.

### 6. Beachhead

The first buyer is a parent who wants more rigorous, focused attention for a child.

Two direct-to-family jobs share one product:

- **Homeschool:** reduce parent burden while preserving parent control and full-program coherence.
- **School supplement:** remediate a struggling learner by ingesting the syllabus, diagnosing gaps, and focusing instruction until progress appears on independent assessments and school work.

The initial Base package is $50 per learner per month for two or three subjects, with included usage and metered overage. The expansion ladder is Core at $100 for full core academics and Premier at $300 for a customized full-spectrum education stack. These are hypotheses, not current revenue.

### 7. Expansion

The adoption ladder is not homeschool now, schools much later. It is overlapping:

1. one family uses Primer;
2. family outcomes and student preference create local advocacy;
3. one student or a bounded cohort becomes a supplementary pilot;
4. Primer launches through LTI with Clever SSO;
5. roster and grade synchronization deepen the deployment;
6. widespread adoption makes Primer the de facto instructional system;
7. Primer earns primary-LMS status rather than demanding replacement on day one.

### 8. Market

Present a vertical expansion, not one blended TAM:

- **Base homeschool:** 3.4 million modeled learners × $600 = **$2.04B** modeled ceiling;
- **Core public-school-family population:** 14.61 million public grades 5–8 learners × $1,200 = **$17.53B** theoretical ceiling;
- **Premier NAIS:** 631,672 learners × $3,600 = **$2.27B** modeled ceiling;
- **Premier premium private:** 1.16 million learners in $15K+ tuition schools × $3,600 = **$4.17B** modeled ceiling.

These layers overlap and must never be summed. Base proves the engine; Core increases curriculum responsibility; Premier consolidates tutoring, coaching, content, and planning spend. The initial SAM still requires grade, subject, willingness-to-pay, supervision, geography, and reachability filters.

### 9. Why now

- Modern models make individualized, conversational feedback economically plausible.
- Generic AI is commoditizing answers, increasing the value of curriculum, governance, and evidence.
- Homeschooling remains structurally larger than before the pandemic.
- Education-choice programmes can expand affordability, though Primer does not require ESA funding initially.
- Schools are actively evaluating AI but need auditable, bounded systems rather than open-ended chat.

### 10. Evidence

Prior research supports the mechanism, not the company outcome:

- peer-reviewed meta-analysis of 89 randomized tutoring trials: approximately 0.29 SD average effect;
- effects are stronger with sustained dosage, alignment, and implementation fidelity;
- mastery and adaptive instruction are promising but produce smaller broad-test effects than narrow aligned assessments;
- Bloom's two-sigma result is historical motivation, not a forecast.

Primer's first falsifiable outcome is progression on independently scored, state-aligned standards. Lack of progression means the system is not working.

### 13. Business model

Initial package ladder:

- **Base, $50/month:** two or three subjects, adaptive tutoring, assessment, and mastery record;
- **Core, $100/month:** full core academics, planning, cross-subject reinforcement, projects, and portfolio;
- **Premier, $300/month:** customized full-spectrum education, managed content, advanced/test-prep/executive-function support, PrimerTV, and higher-touch reporting;
- included model usage plus credit cap or metered overage by tier;
- near-zero human onboarding for Base and Core;
- separate targeted school intervention at approximately $1,200 per supported learner annually;
- premium institutional pricing where Primer becomes learning-support or academic-core infrastructure.

Primary margin risks are inference usage and human support. Do not publish a gross-margin target until observed usage supports it.
