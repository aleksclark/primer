# Content Library — Entertainment & Music/Arts

Evening slot (45–90m, watch-once ledger) + music & arts category. Class: `entertainment` (films/series) and `mixed`/`educational` (music & arts).
Screening criteria: moderate language OK, **violence OK (war films, westerns, battle scenes all fine)**, **progressive/leftist ideological framing is the disqualifier**.

## 1. Classic Films (26)

### Tier A — Start Here (immediately gripping for a 12-year-old boy)

| Film | Year | Why |
|------|------|-----|
| **The Adventures of Robin Hood** | 1938 | Errol Flynn in Technicolor — the definitive swashbuckler; pure chivalric joy |
| **The Great Escape** | 1963 | POW ingenuity, teamwork, McQueen's motorcycle — the gateway classic for boys |
| **The Magnificent Seven** | 1960 | Courage in defense of the weak; Elmer Bernstein's score alone is an education |
| **Shane** | 1953 | The archetypal western through a boy's eyes — the genre's moral weight |
| **20,000 Leagues Under the Sea** | 1954 | Disney's Verne: Nemo, the Nautilus, the squid — adventure plus a real moral puzzle |
| **Treasure Island** | 1950 | Robert Newton's definitive Long John Silver; pairs with the novel |
| **The General** | 1926 | Keaton's Civil War chase masterpiece — proves silent film is thrilling, not homework |
| **Swiss Family Robinson** | 1960 | Resourceful family builds a world from a shipwreck — catnip for a project kid |
| **National Velvet** | 1944 | Grit, family, and the Grand National; wholesome without being saccharine |
| **The Court Jester** | 1955 | Danny Kaye's "vessel with the pestle" — the funniest clean comedy ever filmed |
| **Master and Commander: The Far Side of the World** | 2003 | The modern exception: duty, friendship, seamanship, natural science done seriously |
| **October Sky** | 1999 | Homer Hickam's rockets — self-education, fathers and sons, aspiration |
| **Ben-Hur** | 1959 | ✅ promoted from bench under revised criteria (violence fine): the chariot race, and the most reverent treatment of Christ in Hollywood history |

## 2. Quality Series (11)

| Series | Years | Verdict & Notes |
|--------|-------|-----------------|
| **Hornblower** (ITV) | 1998–2003 | ✅ Top pick. 8 feature-length films from C.S. Forester; duty, courage, growth from midshipman to commander. Naval violence/floggings fine under revised criteria — air the run |
| **Sharpe** (ITV) | 1993–2008 | ⚠ Partially reinstated: violence and language now fine; the remaining issue is **love scenes/nudity** in several episodes, which the revised criteria don't address. Parent screens per-episode or holds for ~14; Hornblower covers the niche meanwhile |

## 3. Music & Arts (10)

| Title | Year(s) | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| **Leonard Bernstein's Young People's Concerts** | 1958–72 | ⭐ The anchor. 53 televised hour programs; Bernstein wrote, conducted, narrated ("What Does Music Mean?", "What Is Sonata Form?"). 5 Emmys. Complete on Kultur DVD Vol. 1 (25 programs) + Vol. 2 (27); many on YouTube. Talks up to kids, never down |
| **Civilisation** (Kenneth Clark, BBC) | 1969 | ⭐ 13 × 50m history of Western art/architecture/ideas; unapologetic defense of the Western inheritance — the most on-mission series in the entire library (cross-listed in history) |
| **Sister Wendy's Story of Painting** (BBC) | 1996 | A nun's luminous, unpretentious art history; warm counterpart to Clark. Painted nudes treated at museum standard |
| **The Joy of Painting** (Bob Ross, PBS) | 1983–94 | Technique, patience, "happy accidents" — calming craft formation; pairs with painting along |
| **Jazz** (Ken Burns, PBS) | 2001 | 10-part history of America's art form — Armstrong, Ellington, Basie; music history as national history. Drugs/vice treated honestly — fine under revised criteria |
| **Fantasia** | 1940 | Disney's animated concert — the classic bridge from cartoons to the concert hall |
| **From Mao to Mozart: Isaac Stern in China** | 1979 | Oscar-winning doc of Stern teaching young Chinese violinists — what mastery and teaching look like; quietly devastating on the Cultural Revolution's cost |
| **The Magic Flute** (Taymor/Met, abridged English) | 2006 | The ideal first opera: fairy-tale plot, puppetry, ~2h in English |
| **Peter and the Wolf** (Templeton stop-motion) | 2006 | Oscar-winning wordless animation of Prokofiev; queue Britten's Young Person's Guide alongside |
| **Landfill Harmonic** | 2015 | Paraguayan kids build an orchestra from trash-dump materials — craftsmanship and music as dignity, not politics |

*Bench: Bernstein's Omnibus lectures (1954–58, save for high school); Karajan Beethoven symphony films; Victory at Sea (1952–53, 26 eps — Richard Rodgers score, doubles as WWII history).*

## Programming notes
- **Evening slot fit**: most films 90–130m (split Kwai / Master and Commander / Ben-Hur across two nights); Twilight Zone/Zorro/Robin Hood are sub-30m filler; Hornblower nights are the movie-length series option.
- **Sequencing**: lead with Tier A adventure, introduce one silent early (The General) to break the "old = boring" barrier, then escalate to Lean, Hitchcock, Capra.
- **Discussion-worthy** (not restrictions — tutor material): High Noon and Kwai endings, 12 Angry Men, Nemo's morality, Patton's ambition.
