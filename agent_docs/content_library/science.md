# Content Library — Science Documentaries

Grade 6 science, Tennessee standards. Class: `educational` (reports instructional hours).
Screening criteria: moderate language OK, violence OK, **progressive/leftist ideological framing is the disqualifier**.

## Life Science (organisms, ecosystems, heredity)

| Title | Year(s) | Eps × Runtime | Why / Tie-in |
|-------|---------|---------------|--------------|
| **Life on Earth** (Attenborough) | 1979 | 13 × 55m | The foundational survey of evolution and the animal kingdom; heredity + classification backbone. Pre-advocacy-era Attenborough — pure natural history |
| **The Living Planet** | 1984 | 12 × 55m | Ecosystems/biomes one per episode — the single best ecosystems series ever made; ideology-free era |
| **The Trials of Life** | 1990 | 12 × 50m | Animal behavior organized by life stage; feeds "organisms and environment" (ties to chicken coop) |
| **The Private Life of Plants** | 1995 | 6 × 50m | Time-lapse botany; direct support for the garden project (growth, reproduction, spacing) |
| **The Life of Birds** | 1998 | 10 × 50m | Behavior, adaptation, flight; obvious tie-in for a kid raising chickens |
| **The Life of Mammals** | 2002 | 10 × 50m | Adaptation and heredity through the mammal orders |
| **Life in the Undergrowth** | 2005 | 5 × 50m | Invertebrates — pairs with garden soil/pollinator observation |
| **Planet Earth** | 2006 | 11 × 50m | Biome-by-biome physical + life science; also the geography backbone. Last of the advocacy-free landmark series |
| **Blue Planet** | 2001 | 8 × 50m | Marine ecosystems, calm pacing, no messaging |
| **Blue Planet II** | 2017 | 7 × 50m | Stronger cinematography; ⚠ **ep 7 "Our Blue Planet" is an environmental-advocacy episode — skip or co-view**; eps 1–6 are straight natural history |
| **Life** | 2009 | 10 × 50m | Survival strategies; strong "adaptation" evidence bank for assessments |
| **Life in the Freezer** | 1993 | 6 × 30m | Antarctica as a complete ecosystem study in short slots — good 25-min morning block |

⚠ **General Attenborough note**: pre-2010 series are pure natural history. Later works (*Our Planet* 2019, *A Life on Our Planet* 2020, *Planet Earth III*) carry heavy climate-advocacy framing — **excluded from this list** for that reason.

## Earth & Space Science (weather, geology, astronomy)

| Title | Year(s) | Eps × Runtime | Why / Tie-in |
|-------|---------|---------------|--------------|
| **Cosmos: A Personal Voyage** (Sagan) | 1980 | 13 × 60m | The gold standard: astronomy + history of science, contemplative pacing; cultural literacy in its own right. Naturalistic worldview but contemplative, not polemical — co-view and discuss where it touches faith |
| **Cosmos: A Spacetime Odyssey** (Tyson) | 2014 | 13 × 44m | ⚠ **Ideology flag**: better orbital-mechanics visuals, but carries explicit anti-religion framing (the Giordano Bruno cartoon in ep 1 is a tendentious church-vs-science morality play) plus climate-advocacy episodes. **Use selected middle episodes only, or prefer Sagan + The Planets instead** |
| **Earth: The Power of the Planet** (Iain Stewart) | 2007 | 5 × 50m | Geology organized by force (volcanoes, atmosphere, ice, oceans) — maps 1:1 to earth-science standards; final ep has mild climate content, minor |
| **The Planets** (BBC, Brian Cox) | 2019 | 5 × 50m | Solar system tour, calm and narratively strong, ideology-free |
| **Wild Weather with Richard Hammond** | 2014 | 3 × 60m | Wind/water/temperature as experiments — direct pre-teach for the weather station build |
| **NOVA: Deadliest Tornadoes / Lightning / Inside the Megastorm** | var. | 1 × 55m each | Cherry-picked meteorology eps; Tennessee severe-weather relevance; weather station tie-in. Avoid NOVA's explicit climate-policy episodes |
| **How the Universe Works** | 2010– | 44m eps | Pick S1–S2 episodes (stars, planets, solar system); later seasons get sensational — curate |

## Physical Science (matter, energy, forces, waves)

| Title | Year(s) | Eps × Runtime | Why / Tie-in |
|-------|---------|---------------|--------------|
| **The Secret Life of Machines** (Tim Hunkin) | 1988–93 | 18 × 25m | Household machines torn down with dry wit and zero flash; Hunkin has blessed free distribution — perfect 25-min slots; electricity/mechanics standards |
| **The Secret Life of Components** (Hunkin, YouTube) | 2021–23 | 15 × ~30m | Workshop-focused sequel; doubles as practical-skills content |
| **The Mechanical Universe** (Caltech) | 1985–86 | 52 × 30m | University physics but the animations (James Blinn) make forces/energy visible; free on Caltech's official YouTube — cherry-pick mechanics/waves eps, stretch material |
| **NOVA: Hunting the Elements** | 2012 | 1 × 110m | Matter/periodic table anchor episode |
| **Richard Hammond's Engineering Connections** | 2008–11 | 26 × 45m | Reverse-engineers landmarks into physics principles; forces standards |
| **Mr. Wizard's World** | 1983–90 | 78 × 26m | Bench-top demonstrations; direct feeder for weather-station instrumentation (also on practical list) |
| **MythBusters** | 2003–16 | ~270 × 44m | Hypothesis → test → revise, modeled constantly; innuendo/bleeped words fine under revised criteria — air broadly, skip only the adult-myth episodes |

## Notes
- **Attenborough priority order**: Living Planet → Life on Earth → Private Life of Plants → Life of Birds (ecosystems and project tie-ins first).
- **Slot fit**: 25–30m items (Life in the Freezer, Secret Life of Machines, Mr. Wizard) fit morning/afternoon blocks; 50–60m items fit the evening documentary slot or split across two mornings.
- **Instructional-hours tagging**: Attenborough → life science standards; Stewart/Cox/Cosmos → earth-space; Hunkin/Mechanical Universe → physical science.
